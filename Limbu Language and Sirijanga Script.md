## Limbu (ᤕᤠᤰᤌᤢᤱ)
Limbu (Yakthung: ᤕᤠᤰᤌᤢᤱ) people are the branch of Sino-Tibetan linguistic family, indigenous communities of Himalayan region of eastern Nepal, India and Bhutan. Also known as Yakthung in their own language. The estimated population of the Limbu is 600,000. Significant numbers of Limbu population are scattered in Sikkim, Darjeeling, Kalimpong, Assam, Nagaland, Myanmar, UK, Hong Kong, United States and other countries.

## Limbu Language
The Limbu language in their native language is called yakthung pan (ᤕᤠᤰᤌᤢᤱ ᤐᤠᤴ). The script to write Limbu language is called Sirijanga script.

## Dialects of the Limbu Language
Like many languages with long history and wide geographic distribution, the Limbu language has several dialects. Each dialect has its own unique vocabulary, pronunciation, and expressions.

Linguists typically recognize for major dialects of the Limbu language:
#### 1. Phedape Dialect
Among four dialects, Phedape dialect is one of the most widely spoken varieties of Yakthnungpan and is generally well understood. It is spoken in areas like Panchthar and Terhathum.
#### 2. Panthare Dialect
Panthare is another major dialect spoken primarily in parts of Panchthar and surrounding regions. Due to its use in literature, education, and cultural publications, it is often regarded as the closest thing to a standard form of written Limbu today. 
#### 3. Chhathare Dialect
The Chhathare dialect is spoken in Dhankuta. It contains distinctive vocabulary and grammatical features that make it somewhat less familiar to speakers of other Limbu dialects.
#### 4. Tamarkhole (Taplejunge) Dialect
The Tamarkhole, sometimes called Taplejunge, dialect is spoken mainly in and around the Taplejung region. It shares similarities with Phedappe but retains unique local characteristics that reflect the history and geography of the area.

Although dialects vary from one region to another, they are all part of the same Yakthungpan language and represent the diversity of the Limbu community.

## What is Sirijanga Script?
Sirijanga Script (also known as Sirijonga, Srijunga, or Sirijanga Lipi) is the traditional writing system used for the Limbu language. The script is considered one of the important indigenous writing systems of the Himalayan region. Which is derived from the Lepcha alphabet by Limbu King Sirijonga Hang in the 9th century.
